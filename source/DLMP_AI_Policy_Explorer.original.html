Executive synthesis · analyst interpretation

Shared aims. Different boundaries.

The selected sources support intentional learning, transparency and human review. Their differences concern who can authorize a use, which work is being assessed, and whether a statement is a recommendation or an enforceable rule.

Learning is more than output.

MIT treats research as an apprenticeship. Cornell protects foundational skills; UNSW distinguishes assessment without AI from assessment of AI competence. The DLMP question is which reasoning learners must practice, explain and retain.

MIT ↗Cornell University ↗UNSW Sydney ↗

Permission has a scope.

Duke’s default prohibits assignment completion without express permission. Cambridge permits appropriate personal study but limits unacknowledged summative use. Brown proposes a human-assistance baseline. These are different activities and levels of authority, not a simple permissive-to-restrictive ranking.

Duke University ↗Cambridge ↗Cambridge ↗Brown University ↗

Approval is not authorization.

UW Medicine §E requires learner authorization for specified activities even with approved or low-risk tools. Output review remains the user’s responsibility. A disclosed tool or a redacted input does not itself resolve institutional permission.

UW Medicine ↗UW Medicine ↗University of Pennsylvania ↗
